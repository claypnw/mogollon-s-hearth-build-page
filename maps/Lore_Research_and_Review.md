# Nirn Atlas Lore Pop-ups

42 lore cards across the World of Nirn and Skyrim maps

The two paragraphs under each location are the atlas pop-up copy. Source links sit below the copy for review. Skyrim, High Rock, Hammerfell, Cyrodiil, and Morrowind use the same entries on both maps. The Skyrim entries use the Fourth Era setting of *The Elder Scrolls V* as their main reference point. Earlier events are dated or identified by era where relevant. The copy puts the location and its defining features first for tablet and phone readers.

## World of Nirn

### Tamriel

Tamriel is the continent at the heart of the Elder Scrolls games. Its lands stretch from the icy shores of Skyrim to the wetlands of Black Marsh and the forests of Valenwood. Cyrodiil lies at the center, where the Imperial City rises from Lake Rumare. Offshore, the Summerset Isles form the ancestral home of the High Elves.

The familiar nine-province map reflects an Imperial view of the continent. Political loyalties shift across the eras. In the Second Era, three alliances fight over Cyrodiil and the Ruby Throne. Centuries later, the Empire again faces challenges to its authority. The kingdoms, clans, and cities on the map each have histories that extend beyond Imperial rule.

### Skyrim

Skyrim is the northern homeland of the Nords. Nine holds divide its lands, from the marshes around Morthal and the open tundra of Whiterun to the snowy coast of Winterhold. Nordic barrows preserve the dead of earlier ages, and the Greybeards keep the tradition of the Voice at High Hrothgar, on the slopes of the Throat of the World.

In the Fourth Era, the death of High King Torygg sets Skyrim’s civil war in motion. The Imperial Legion and Ulfric Stormcloak’s followers compete for the province as dragons return to the skies. Into that turmoil comes the Last Dragonborn, a mortal with a dragon’s soul and the power to wield the Voice.

**Sources** · [Bethesda on Skyrim’s setting and story](https://elderscrolls.bethesda.net/en-US/news/ppRNIJ9N60w88gCYqIswW/what-makes-the-elder-scrolls-v-skyrim-special) · [Prima’s guide to the nine holds](https://primagames.com/eguides/elder-scrolls-v-skyrim-special-edition-eguide/Training/training-part-3-adventuring-across-skyrim/holds-skyrim)

### High Rock

High Rock occupies northwestern Tamriel, along the northern shores of the Iliac Bay. It is the homeland of the Bretons, and much of its history belongs to individual kingdoms and their noble houses. Daggerfall and Wayrest are two of the best-known cities on the bay. The coast links them to their neighbors in Hammerfell through trade, diplomacy, and war.

In the Third Era, the discovery of the ancient golem Numidium sends rival powers scrambling for control of the Iliac Bay. The Emperor’s agent is dispatched to investigate the restless spirit of Daggerfall’s dead king, drawing a local haunting into a struggle over one of the Empire’s most powerful weapons.

### Hammerfell

Hammerfell is the western homeland of the Redguards, whose ancestors crossed the sea from Yokuda. The Alik’r Desert covers much of the northwest, where the port of Sentinel looks across the Iliac Bay. Farther south, Hew’s Bane and the harbor city of Abah’s Landing show how much of Hammerfell’s life also depends on the coast.

The early Yokudan fighting forces were known as the Ra Gada, a name associated with the origins of “Redguard.” Later generations fought over the province’s place in the Empire. In the Fourth Era, Hammerfell rejected the territorial concessions of the White-Gold Concordat and continued fighting the Aldmeri Dominion before a separate treaty ended the war there.

**Sources** · [ESO Thieves Guild and Hew’s Bane](https://www.elderscrollsonline.com/en-us/updates/dlc/thievesguild) · [Third Pocket Guide, Other Lands](https://elderscrolls.fandom.com/wiki/Pocket_Guide_to_the_Empire,_Third_Edition:_Other_Lands) · [The Great War, in-game book](https://skyrim.fandom.com/wiki/The_Great_War)

### Cyrodiil

Cyrodiil is the heartland of the Empire and the homeland of the Imperials. At its center stands the Imperial City, built on an island in Lake Rumare. The White-Gold Tower, a legacy of the ancient Ayleids, rises above the capital. The province reaches from the Jerall Mountains on Skyrim’s border to the Gold Coast in the west and the Nibenay Basin in the east.

Its capital has been a prize for rival rulers across the eras. In the Second Era, three alliances fought to claim the Ruby Throne. During the Third Era’s Oblivion Crisis, the murder of Emperor Uriel Septim VII left the Empire facing open gates to Oblivion.

**Sources** · [Bethesda’s Oblivion Remastered overview](https://elderscrolls.bethesda.net/en-US/oblivion-remastered) · [ESO Imperial City](https://www.elderscrollsonline.com/en-gb/updates/dlc/imperialcity)

### Morrowind

Morrowind is the northeastern homeland of the Dunmer, or Dark Elves. It includes a large mainland as well as the volcanic island of Vvardenfell. Its settlements reflect competing traditions, from the political power of the Great Houses to the religious and ancestral customs of Ashlander communities. For much of its history, the Tribunal of Almalexia, Sotha Sil, and Vivec shaped public worship.

At the beginning of the Fourth Era, Red Mountain erupted in the disaster remembered as the Red Year. Ash and destruction spread across Vvardenfell, and refugees sought homes elsewhere, including in Skyrim and on Solstheim. The eruption reshaped both Morrowind’s landscape and the lives of Dunmer communities far from it.

### Summerset Isles

The Summerset Isles lie off southwestern Tamriel and are the ancestral homeland of the Altmer, or High Elves. Summerset and Auridon are the principal islands. Alinor, on the larger island, is a seat of political power. The archipelago also has close ties to the Psijic Order, a community of mages based on the elusive island of Artaeum.

In the Second Era, Queen Ayrenn opened Summerset’s long-closed borders to foreign visitors. The decision brought outsiders into cities known for their towers, gardens, and formal traditions. In the Fourth Era, the islands became the center of the Thalmor-led Aldmeri Dominion, whose influence extended far beyond the shores of Summerset.

### Valenwood

Valenwood is the densely forested homeland of the Bosmer, or Wood Elves, in southwestern Tamriel. Some Bosmer settlements are built into living trees. Elden Root, in Grahtwood, grew around an enormous graht-oak and became the center of the Aldmeri Dominion during the Second Era. Other parts of the province hold Ayleid ruins, trading posts, and settlements tucked into the forest.

Bosmer tradition traces the Green Pact to Y’ffre. Its obligations protect the living forest and shape the practices of Bosmer communities, including what they eat and how they build. Local interpretations vary, but the pact helps explain why living trees can serve as homes and why timber from beyond Valenwood has value.

### Elsweyr

Elsweyr is the southern homeland of the Khajiit. In the north, the dry lands of Anequina surround the city of Rimmen. To the south, Pellitine reaches through the forests toward the port of Senchal. The phases of Nirn’s two moons at birth are central to Khajiiti tradition and determine the forms, or furstocks, Khajiit take.

Anequina and Pellitine were joined during the Second Era, though each kept a distinct regional identity. In that era, dragons were unleashed across Elsweyr, threatening settlements in both north and south. The struggle brought together local defenders and the Dragonguard, whose traditions were shaped by centuries of studying and fighting dragons.

### Black Marsh

Black Marsh, also called Argonia, lies in southeastern Tamriel. It is home to the Argonians, many of whom call themselves Saxhleel. Rivers and wetlands make travel through the interior difficult for outsiders. In the coastal region of Murkmire, the port of Lilmoth gives visitors a point of entry into country shaped by water, dense growth, and ancient ruins.

Many Argonian communities maintain deep ties to the Hist, living trees with a role in both communal life and spiritual tradition. Black Marsh also preserves the stone pyramids, or xanmeers, of earlier Argonian builders. The ruins and the living traditions around them offer different views of the region’s long history.

### Atmora

Atmora lies across the Sea of Ghosts, north of Tamriel. Nordic tradition remembers it as an ancestral homeland and the place from which Ysgramor sailed. Stories of Saarthal, the Night of Tears, and the return of Ysgramor’s Five Hundred Companions tell of one famous chapter in a much longer history of movement between Atmora and Skyrim.

The Imperial *Pocket Guide* reports that later expeditions found an increasingly cold and inhospitable land. Its account leaves much of Atmora’s interior and later history uncertain. In Skyrim, however, the memory of Atmora survives in the stories of the Companions and in the old barrows and settlements associated with the first Nordic communities.

**Sources** · [Third Pocket Guide, Other Lands](https://elderscrolls.fandom.com/wiki/Pocket_Guide_to_the_Empire,_Third_Edition:_Other_Lands) · [Songs of the Return, in-game book series](https://skyrim.fandom.com/wiki/Songs_of_the_Return)

### Akavir

Akavir lies across the eastern ocean from Tamriel. Imperial texts call it the Dragon Land and describe peoples including the Tsaesci, the Tang Mo, the Kamal, and the Ka Po’ Tun. Tamrielic scholars caution that many descriptions come through older accounts or translations, leaving the details of Akavir’s peoples and geography open to interpretation.

Its influence on Tamriel is easier to trace. A First Era Tsaesci invasion ended at Pale Pass, and Akaviri figures later served the Reman Empire and ruled as Potentates. In the Third Era, Emperor Uriel Septim V led an expedition in the opposite direction. It ended at Ionith, where the emperor died and the invasion collapsed.

**Sources** · [Third Pocket Guide, Other Lands](https://elderscrolls.fandom.com/wiki/Pocket_Guide_to_the_Empire,_Third_Edition:_Other_Lands) · [Mysterious Akavir, in-game book](https://elderscrolls.fandom.com/wiki/Mysterious_Akavir) · [Report on the Disaster at Ionith, in-game book](https://skyrimbooksproj.web.app/books/notes-letters/report-disaster-at-ionith)

### Yokuda

Yokuda is the ancestral homeland of the Redguards, across the western sea from Tamriel. The Imperial *Pocket Guide* describes a country with rocky hills, skilled farmers, and a long history of warfare. Many of its people crossed to Tamriel after a catastrophe destroyed much of the old land, carrying memories and traditions that survived in Hammerfell.

Accounts disagree about what caused Yokuda’s destruction. Some connect it to civil conflict and the sword-singers, warriors whose traditions included manifesting a spirit sword. The old homeland nevertheless remained central to Redguard identity. Its stories help explain how a people who had lost so much of their country built a new homeland on Tamriel’s western shore.

**Sources** · [Third Pocket Guide, Other Lands](https://elderscrolls.fandom.com/wiki/Pocket_Guide_to_the_Empire,_Third_Edition:_Other_Lands) · [The Lost Islands of Old Yokuda, in-game book](https://eso.guilds.tr/en/books/the-lost-islands-of-old-yokuda)

### Pyandonea

Pyandonea is the island homeland of the Maormer, or Sea Elves. The First Edition of the Imperial *Pocket Guide* places it far south of Summerset. Later accounts describe a mist-covered land and tell of a surviving Altmer ship that brought home stories of tangled sea vegetation, treacherous channels, and serpents used by the Maormer.

Pyandonea’s ruler, King Orgnum, is a figure of legend as well as a recurring adversary in Summerset’s histories. In the Third Era’s War of the Isle, a combined defense stopped his invading fleet. The *Pocket Guide* credits a storm raised by the Psijic Order with helping decide the battle and leaves the fate of Orgnum’s power uncertain.

### Solstheim

Solstheim lies off the coast of Morrowind in the Sea of Ghosts. The island changes sharply from the ash-covered country around Raven Rock to snowy forests and mountains farther north. Raven Rock is home to a Dunmer community, while the Skaal maintain their own traditions in a village on the island’s northeastern side.

Visitors in the late Third Era saw an Imperial mining settlement taking shape at Raven Rock. By the time of Skyrim’s Fourth Era, the town was governed by House Redoran, and ash from Red Mountain had transformed the island’s south. The ruins of an older dragon cult also tie Solstheim to Miraak, an ancient Dragonborn remembered by the Skaal.

**Sources** · [Bethesda’s Dragonborn overview](https://elderscrolls.bethesda.net/en-US/skyrim10) · [UESP Morrowind Lore Book](https://esofiles.uesp.net/misc/TestLoreBook1.pdf) · [History of Raven Rock, in-game book series](https://elderscrolls.fandom.com/wiki/History_of_Raven_Rock)

### Vvardenfell

Vvardenfell is the great volcanic island within Morrowind’s Inner Sea. Red Mountain dominates its center, but the island also has wetlands, greener coastal country, and forests of giant fungi. Settlements range from the canton city of Vivec to the villages of the Bitter Coast. Dwemer ruins and the camps of Ashlander communities preserve other layers of its history.

In the late Third Era, the Blight spread from Red Mountain while the figure known as the Nerevarine confronted Dagoth Ur. Early in the Fourth Era, the volcano erupted in the Red Year. The catastrophe altered the island’s settlements and sent many Dunmer looking for safety on the mainland and beyond.

**Sources** · [Bethesda’s Morrowind overview on the Nerevarine prophecy](https://elderscrolls.bethesda.net/en-US/morrowind) · [ESO Morrowind](https://www.elderscrollsonline.com/en-us/updates/chapter/morrowind) · [UESP Morrowind Lore Book](https://esofiles.uesp.net/misc/TestLoreBook1.pdf) · [The Red Year, in-game book](https://elderscrolls.fandom.com/wiki/The_Red_Year)

### Abecean Sea

The Abecean Sea is a pocket of the western Eltheric Ocean. An in-game zone description places it south of Hammerfell, west of Cyrodiil’s Gold Coast, north of Valenwood, and northeast of Summerset. These shores bring together the ports and waterways of four different provinces, and the same sea can be approached from both the mainland and the islands.

One of its best-known islands is Stros M’Kai, the setting of *The Elder Scrolls Adventures: Redguard*. In the Second Era, the Redguard adventurer Cyrus arrived there looking for his sister. His search drew him into the island’s resistance to Imperial occupation, a reminder of how much a single harbor could matter in the struggle for Hammerfell.

**Sources** · [Abecean Sea, ESO zone text transcribed by ESO Hub](https://eso-hub.com/en/zones/greenshade/abecean-sea) · [Bethesda’s Redguard overview](https://elderscrolls.bethesda.net/en-US/redguard)

### Eltheric Ocean

The Eltheric Ocean is the broad expanse west of Tamriel. It includes the Abecean Sea, which runs between several southwestern shores, and the more distant Sea of Pearls. From the coast, the same western waters lead toward island chains such as the Systres and toward the surviving islands associated with old Yokuda.

The crossing from Yokuda to Hammerfell was one of the most consequential voyages in Tamriel’s history. In the First Era, Yokudan migrants carried their traditions across the western waters and established a new homeland on the mainland. Later stories of the ocean also describe the Sload of Thras, whose wars and plague left a very different mark on Tamriel.

**Sources** · [Abecean Sea, ESO zone text transcribed by ESO Hub](https://eso-hub.com/en/zones/greenshade/abecean-sea) · [ESO High Isle and the Systres](https://www.elderscrollsonline.com/en-us/updates/chapter/highisle) · [Third Pocket Guide, Other Lands](https://elderscrolls.fandom.com/wiki/Pocket_Guide_to_the_Empire,_Third_Edition:_Other_Lands)

### Sea of Ghosts

The Sea of Ghosts washes Skyrim’s northern shore and extends east toward Morrowind. It surrounds Solstheim and lies between Skyrim and Atmora. Its coast includes the harbor at Dawnstar and the cliffs below Winterhold. Ice, rocky shores, and the remains of wrecked ships make the northern sea a conspicuous part of the landscape in *Skyrim*.

It also forms the setting for two very different chapters of Nordic history. The voyages of Ysgramor and the Companions link the sea to the settlement of Skyrim. Much later, the Great Collapse sent most of the town of Winterhold into these waters, leaving the College standing above the coast.

**Sources** · [Third Pocket Guide, Other Lands](https://elderscrolls.fandom.com/wiki/Pocket_Guide_to_the_Empire,_Third_Edition:_Other_Lands) · [The Great Collapse](https://elderscrolls.fandom.com/wiki/The_Great_Collapse) · [UESP Morrowind Lore Book](https://esofiles.uesp.net/misc/UESP_Lore_Book_RC3.pdf)

### Inner Sea

The Inner Sea is the broad, bay-like body of water between Vvardenfell and the mainland of Morrowind. It wraps around the island’s southern and western shores. The volcanic island occupies the middle of this partly enclosed stretch of water, with the mainland close enough for crossings between coastal settlements to play an everyday part in travel.

The shores help explain how Vvardenfell fits into the larger province. Vivec City’s cantons rise above the water, and the nearby harbor at Ebonheart served as a seat of Imperial administration in the late Third Era. The same coastline brings the city, the island’s smaller communities, and mainland Morrowind into close contact.

**Sources** · [UESP Morrowind Lore Book, geography](https://esofiles.uesp.net/misc/TestLoreBook1.pdf) · [ESO Morrowind](https://www.elderscrollsonline.com/en-us/updates/chapter/morrowind) · [Ebonheart in Morrowind](https://elderscrolls.fandom.com/wiki/Ebonheart_(Morrowind))

### Padomaic Ocean

The Padomaic Ocean is the vast eastern water between Tamriel and Akavir. Much of what Tamrielic writers knew about the crossing came from contact with Akaviri sailors, invaders, and later settlers. The lands on the far side were familiar enough to appear in histories, yet those histories still left large gaps in what people knew about their geography.

Emperor Uriel Septim V tested that knowledge with a Third Era invasion of Akavir. The campaign depended on moving soldiers and supplies across the sea and holding ports on the far coast. The expedition ended in disaster at Ionith, leaving an unusually detailed Imperial record of a crossing that otherwise appears mostly in accounts of raids and invasions.

**Sources** · [Third Pocket Guide, Other Lands](https://elderscrolls.fandom.com/wiki/Pocket_Guide_to_the_Empire,_Third_Edition:_Other_Lands) · [Report on the Disaster at Ionith, in-game book](https://skyrimbooksproj.web.app/books/notes-letters/report-disaster-at-ionith)

### Sea of Pearls

The Sea of Pearls lies in the western waters of Nirn. An in-game account attributed to Hazadiyya Sea-Queen links it to Samara, one of the islands of old Yokuda. Tamrielic accounts also associate the sea with Thras, the island realm of the Sload. Its exact limits are unclear, and much of its lore comes from the stories of individual sailors.

One such story comes from Daggerfall. According to the description of Captain Margaux’s home near the docks, the Breton merchant and explorer sailed into the Sea of Pearls aboard the *Golden Spriggan* and was never seen again. Other accounts attach sea monsters and submerged realms to these waters. Margaux’s story leaves the fate of her ship and crew open.

## Skyrim

The entries below cover the nine holds and their marked capitals, the Velothi Mountains, and Mogollon Hearth. The province label and the neighboring labels for High Rock, Hammerfell, Cyrodiil, and Morrowind use their entries above.

### Haafingar Hold

Haafingar occupies Skyrim’s northwestern corner, where mountains descend toward the Sea of Ghosts. Solitude, its capital, overlooks the mouth of the Karth River. The road through Dragon Bridge links the port to the interior. Along the coast and in the mountains, old ruins and isolated strongholds mark ground far removed from the bustle of the capital.

Haafingar’s political importance comes from Solitude. In the Second Era, the city served as the capital of Western Skyrim. In the Fourth Era, its harbor and fortifications supported the Imperial Legion’s position during the civil war. The hold’s landscape gives that power a visible center, with the city standing high above the ships and roads that supply it.

### Solitude

Solitude stands on a natural stone arch overlooking the Karth River and its harbor. The Blue Palace is the seat of the jarl, while Castle Dour houses the Imperial Legion’s command in Skyrim. The city is also home to the Bards College, whose students preserve songs and stories alongside the city’s more formal court traditions.

In the Fourth Era, Solitude became a center of the conflict between the Empire and the Stormcloaks. Jarl Elisif the Fair governs the city after the death of High King Torygg, her husband. Below the walls, ships still use the docks, and above them the annual Burning of King Olaf keeps an older local story in public view.

### Hjaalmarch Hold

Hjaalmarch lies in northwestern Skyrim. Its low country includes the salt marsh around Morthal, where islands and channels break up the ground between the town and the coast. The hold also reaches into higher terrain, with wooded slopes and rocky passages leading away from the fog of the marsh.

South of Morthal lies Labyrinthian, one of Skyrim’s major Nordic ruin complexes. The site preserves traces of the old city of Bromjunaar and the age of the dragon priests. Its ruins and Shalidor’s Maze show how later mages and explorers also left their mark on a landscape already filled with reminders of Skyrim’s early history.

**Sources** · [ESO Greymoor on Western Skyrim](https://www.elderscrollsonline.com/en-us/updates/chapter/greymoor) · [Prima’s Skyrim atlas for Hjaalmarch Hold](https://primagames.com/eguides/elder-scrolls-v-skyrim-special-edition-eguide/the-atlas/hold-2-hjaalmarch/overview) · [UESP’s Greymoor developer discussion notes](https://blog.uesp.net/eso-live-2020-05-22-art-and-lore-of-greymoor/) · [Labyrinthian in Skyrim](https://skyrimmap.com/location/labyrinthian)

### Morthal

Morthal is the small capital of Hjaalmarch, built at the edge of a broad marsh. Its timber houses sit near damp ground and shallow water, with the jarl’s hall overlooking the settlement. During the events of *Skyrim*, Jarl Idgrod Ravencrone governs the town and has a reputation for visions that some neighbors trust and others find unsettling.

A burned house is at the center of one of Morthal’s local mysteries. Its destruction, and the deaths connected to it, force the town to reckon with what has been happening beyond its lights. The town’s secluded setting also attracts Falion, a mage whose interests are a source of rumor among his neighbors.

### The Pale Hold

The Pale stretches from Dawnstar’s northern coast into Skyrim’s interior. Its snow-covered heights give way to woodland farther south. The roads and passes through this long hold connect the coast to inland Skyrim, while mines near Dawnstar have provided work close to the shore.

The hold contains traces of several periods of Nordic history. Korvanjund is a burial ruin linked to the Jagged Crown, an emblem of kingship sought by both sides in the Fourth Era civil war. Near Dawnstar, Nightcaller Temple preserves a darker history tied to Vaermina. These sites put the old struggles over faith and authority within reach of the same northern port.

### Dawnstar

Dawnstar is the capital of the Pale and a harbor on the Sea of Ghosts. The settlement stretches around the water, with homes, docks, and two mines nearby. The Iron-Breaker and Quicksilver mines give Dawnstar a livelihood alongside its maritime trade. Snow and exposed rock begin just beyond the town’s cluster of buildings.

In the Fourth Era, residents report nightmares that keep them from sleeping. Their trouble draws attention to Nightcaller Temple, an old sanctuary of Vaermina above the town. Dawnstar also houses a private museum devoted to the Mythic Dawn, the cult implicated in the Oblivion Crisis. Its collection brings one of the Empire’s darkest episodes into a small northern port.

### Winterhold Hold

Winterhold Hold covers a stretch of Skyrim’s far northeastern coast. Snow, wind, and exposed slopes give much of the hold a stark appearance. Its capital looks out over the Sea of Ghosts, while the country inland contains Nordic tombs and places tied to Skyrim’s early settlers. Winterhold is one of the four regions traditionally called Skyrim’s Old Holds.

Saarthal, southwest of the town, is remembered in Nordic accounts as one of the earliest settlements of the Atmoran arrivals. The Night of Tears made it central to later stories of conflict with the Snow Elves. In the Fourth Era, the College of Winterhold conducts excavations among the ruins.

### Winterhold

Winterhold was once a major city on Skyrim’s northern coast. In 4E 122, the Great Collapse sent much of the settlement over the cliffs and into the Sea of Ghosts. A small town survived, and the College of Winterhold still stands above the water at the end of a narrow stone bridge.

The College draws mages to study in a place where magic remains a source of local suspicion. Some townspeople blame it for the Collapse, while the mages dispute that account. The gap between the remaining town and the College is visible in the landscape itself, where the bridge crosses the ground lost to the sea.

### Eastmarch Hold

Eastmarch lies in eastern Skyrim, with Windhelm at its northern end and a broad basin of hot springs farther south. Steam rises from pools in the volcanic tundra, even while the country near Windhelm lies under snow. The White River runs through the hold toward the coast, linking the city’s harbor to the country upriver.

Eastmarch has been a center of Nordic power across several eras. During the Second Era, Windhelm was a seat of the eastern kingdom. In the Fourth Era, its jarl, Ulfric Stormcloak, led the rebellion against Imperial rule. The rest of the hold includes mills, farms, and settlements that give Eastmarch a life beyond its capital.

### Windhelm

Windhelm stands above the White River in eastern Skyrim. Nordic tradition attributes its founding to Ysgramor, and the massive walls and Palace of the Kings reflect its long association with Nordic rule. The city’s docks connect it to the Sea of Ghosts, while the stone bridge carries travelers through the main gate.

In the Fourth Era, Ulfric Stormcloak rules from the palace and makes Windhelm the center of his rebellion. The city is also home to Dunmer residents in the Gray Quarter and Argonian workers at the docks. The different places those communities can live and work show how unevenly the city has welcomed its newer residents.

### Whiterun Hold

Whiterun Hold occupies the center of Skyrim. Open tundra stretches around the capital, and farms cluster near the city roads. The hold also reaches into the uplands around the Throat of the World. The Riverwood valley and the plains west toward Rorikstead give travelers two very different views of the province’s interior.

The roads across the hold give it strategic importance in the Fourth Era civil war. Jarl Balgruuf initially tries to keep Whiterun out of the conflict as both sides look to control its central routes. An older source of influence stands above the roads at High Hrothgar, where the Greybeards practice and teach the Way of the Voice.

### Whiterun

Whiterun rises above the central plains, with farms around its walls and market stalls near the main gate. Dragonsreach overlooks the city from the top of the hill. Below it are the Gildergreen, a tree sacred to followers of Kynareth, and Jorrvaskr, the hall of the Companions. The Skyforge stands above Jorrvaskr and has a place in the order’s traditions.

The city’s old stories include the capture of a dragon at Dragonsreach, an episode associated with King Olaf One-Eye. By the Fourth Era, Jarl Balgruuf governs from the same hall, and the return of dragons brings the old story back into public life.

### The Reach Hold

The Reach is a rugged western region cut by the Karth River and bordered by high mountains. Roads follow narrow valleys past mines, stone towers, and older ruins. Markarth is the chief city, but the mountains and valleys are also the homeland of the Reachfolk, whose clans have their own traditions and histories of rule.

In the Second Era, Markarth was a center of Reachfolk power. By the time of *Skyrim*, the hold is administered by a jarl, and the Forsworn fight to reclaim Reachfolk rule. The Fourth Era struggle is rooted in the contested control of Markarth and the surrounding land, where imperial politics and local claims meet.

### Markarth

Markarth is built into the rock of the Reach on the site of the old Dwemer city of Nchuand-Zel. Water runs through the settlement below steep terraces and stone stairways. The jarl’s seat is Understone Keep, and the old Dwemer halls beneath and around it show how much of the modern city occupies a much older foundation.

Silver shapes much of the city’s Fourth Era life. The Silver-Blood family’s influence reaches through the mines and into local politics, and Cidhna Mine serves as a prison as well as a source of ore. The conflicts surrounding the mine bring the city’s wealth and its struggle over Reachfolk rule into the same narrow streets.

### Falkreath Hold

Falkreath Hold lies in southern Skyrim, where pine forests surround Lake Ilinalta and roads climb toward the Cyrodiil border. Its terrain is greener and more heavily wooded than the northern holds. Falkreath is the capital, and Helgen sits farther east near the route through the mountains.

The hold’s position has put it in the path of conflicts across the eras. In the Second Era, the town of Falkreath came under attack by forces led by the minotaur Domihaus. At the opening of *Skyrim*, the Fourth Era return of dragons becomes impossible to ignore when Alduin attacks Helgen and disrupts an Imperial execution there.

### Falkreath

Falkreath is a small town amid the pine forests of southern Skyrim. It serves as the hold’s seat, and its large graveyard gives the town a reputation disproportionate to its size. Its dead include people associated with wars fought in and around the region. Names such as the Dead Man’s Drink carry that history into everyday town life.

The roads near Falkreath lead toward Lake Ilinalta and the mountains along the Cyrodiil border. In the Second Era, the town was the target of an assault led by Domihaus. By the Fourth Era, it appears as a quieter place, with a sawmill and the graveyard among its most prominent landmarks.

### The Rift Hold

The Rift is Skyrim’s southeastern hold, a high country of lakes, cliffs, and woods with autumn-colored leaves. Riften stands at Lake Honrich, while Ivarstead and Shor’s Stone anchor smaller communities along the hold’s roads. At the western edge, the ascent from Ivarstead leads toward High Hrothgar and the Greybeards.

Trade and travel give the Rift several distinct centers. Riften’s docks and market serve the lake country, and Shor’s Stone has grown around mining. The hold’s forests and slopes also contain old Nordic ruins and isolated settlements. Their presence gives the Rift a history beyond the politics and commerce of its capital.

### Riften

Riften stands at the eastern end of Lake Honrich, in Skyrim’s southeastern Rift. Timber buildings overlook the canal, and a market occupies the center of town. The docks connect the city to the lake, while Mistveil Keep houses the jarl. Beneath the streets, the tunnels called the Ratway give the city another level of life.

During the Fourth Era, Maven Black-Briar and her family wield influence through their mead business and political ties. The Thieves Guild operates below the city, with the Ragged Flagon serving as its gathering place. The market, the meadery, and the Ratway show three sides of the same lakeside trading town.

### Velothi Mountains

The Velothi Mountains form much of the boundary between Skyrim and Morrowind. Seen from Skyrim, their ridges rise beyond Eastmarch and the Rift. The mountains divide the two provinces, while the passes through them funnel movement into a handful of routes. Dunmeth Pass is one such route near Windhelm.

The range takes its name from Veloth, the prophet associated with the migration of the Chimer, ancestors of the Dunmer, to Morrowind. Far later, the Red Year drove Dunmer refugees into Skyrim. Windhelm’s Dunmer community is one reminder that the border has a history of migration as well as the separation visible on the map.

### Mogollon Hearth

South-southeast of Riften, the mountains rise above the woods and roads of the Rift. Mogollon Hearth is the name used in this atlas for a stretch of those foothills. The shaded area marks the country around it. Think of the Hearth as a place travelers might know through the directions of someone in Riften or a story heard along the road.

The Hearth belongs to this atlas’s own Skyrim setting. Its real-world inspiration is the high country around Payson, Arizona, where pine forests and steep ridges give the place its character. In the atlas, the Hearth is a place to arrive, get warm, and hear what lies beyond the next ridge.
